## 2. New (Claude, PROPOSED): real local data, (L) and the discriminant

**What (L) is** (HCG v0.2 ADOPTED §6.9 and §6.11, PO-2 and PO-4):
- (L) means e_σ ∈ L(D), the lattice generated by admissible Schoen suppliers.
- **PO-4** (Severn [387] Thm 3.1, CHECKED): *if every local monodromy is conjugate to its inverse, then (L) holds.* G₃₄ is the instance of this.

The question ChatGPT proposes, (L) + (D) + (H) ⇒? split, therefore contains as its main sub-case: **do "real" local data (every g_j ~ g_j⁻¹ in Γ) force a split discriminant?**

**Lemma R′ (reduction for real local monodromies; proved, PROPOSED).** Let g be conjugate to g⁻¹ by a K-linear h₀-isometry u; in Hurwitz data this means Γ-conjugate. Decompose V by the K-irreducible factors of the characteristic polynomial; these are the factors of the cyclotomic polynomials Φₙ.
- **3 | n.** Then Q(ζₙ) ⊇ K, so Φₙ = f·f̄ over K, and inversion (complex conjugation on roots of unity) swaps f and f̄. So u carries E_f onto E_f̄. Their Gram determinants are equal, and their b-parts are c and c̄, so together they contribute only norms.
- **3 ∤ n, n ≥ 2.** Then Φₙ stays irreducible and self-reciprocal over K, and u preserves E_{Φₙ}.

Hence

  **[a(g) b(g)] = [Π_{n ≥ 2, 3 ∤ n} disc(E_{Φₙ}(g)) · Φₙ(1)^{dim E_{Φₙ}/φ(n)}]**,

where Φ₂(1) = 2, and Φₙ(1) = p for n = p^k, otherwise 1.

**D-24 (Claude, concession, caught by my own check before sending).** My first version kept only the factor x + 1, i.e. [disc(E₋₁)·2^{dim E₋₁}]. It is **false**. The order-4 element e₁ ↦ −e₃ ↦ −e₁ (fixing e₂) in G(6,1,3) is real with eigenvalues ±i ∉ K. Its factor Φ₄ = x² + 1 contributes (1 − i)(1 + i) = 2, a non-norm, although E₋₁ = 0.

- **Lemma R′ holds on 38/38 real classes** of the five groups tested (`gap/real_reduction_check.g`).

**Two easy closed cases (proved).**
- Rank-one real data: characters of order ≤ 2, with an even number of −1's by product-one, so the product is 2^{even}·1 = [1].
- Monomial data in G(m, p, n) with the standard form and only order-2 reflections: these are split.
  - Transposition-type reflections have roots of norm 2, so local factor [2·2] = [1].

## 3. Proposed next steps for the obstruction track

- **Revise the track's first target.** "(L) ⇒ split" is false at the class-datum level (witness above). The meaningful question is how dominance interacts with the local class product:
  - Can a nonsplit datum satisfy N ≥ 19 together with the rank budget at all? Under (H) this currently needs d ≥ 53.
  - Is there a d-independent reason it cannot?
- **Use Lemma R′ as a gate for real data.** A nonsplit real datum needs self-reciprocal factors Φₙ (3 ∤ n) whose eigenspace discriminants times Φₙ(1)^{…} multiply to a non-norm. Φₙ(1) is a non-norm only for n = p^k with p ≡ 2 (mod 3), including p = 2. This is checkable from local spectra and Gram data alone.
- **Keep (L) and "split" as separate gates** in the certificate. Record example 11 as the separating witness (toy, fails (D)), alongside the C₆ separator (fails (L) and (D)) and G₃₄ (passes all, split).
- **Possible direct use.** Try to build the same mechanism (Φ₄ factors with odd total contribution) inside a group large enough to meet N ≥ 19. That is a concrete search for a nonsplit candidate that does not rely on the d-by-d screen, and it would have to respect d ≥ 53 under (H).
