# RTG and the Hodge Conjecture: a concrete mathematical route

Yes. We can use the RTG sixfold dyadic structure to construct an explicit projective algebraic variety, calculate its Hodge decomposition, and identify algebraic cycles representing its rational Hodge classes.

This is substantially more useful than simply observing that RTG contains the imaginary unit \\(i\\).

However, there is a critical boundary: this construction would demonstrate a known special case of the Hodge Conjecture, not prove the general conjecture. The general problem remains unsolved as of October 2026.&#x20;

I see three levels of investigation:

1. Immediate, rigorous: Construct an elliptic curve from the RTG dyadic operator \\(A\\), its B-compatible complex structure \\(J_Q\\), and an explicitly chosen integer lattice.
2. Substantive extension: Build products of those curves, calculate their rational Hodge classes, and exhibit algebraic cycles generating them.
3. Longer-term research: Investigate whether RTG supplies a nontrivial selection principle for complex structures, integral lattices, or algebraic cycles beyond what standard algebraic geometry already provides.

There is also a distinction in the RTG documentation we must preserve.

## 1. RTG already has a Hodge decomposition—but not the one in the conjecture

[OWNER: Pre-Legacy v0.8 §1.9; OPEN-M v0.7.1 §6, `UVM-NUM-2D-HODGE`]

The ESM/OPEN-M studies use a discrete Hodge decomposition of a field on a cell complex:

\\[ z=z\_{\rm exact}+z\_{\rm coexact}+z\_{\rm harmonic}. \\]

The harmonic sector is related to the topology of that complex, including its first Betti number \\(\beta_1\\).

The Hodge Conjecture, however, concerns an entirely different decomposition:

\\[ H^m(X,\mathbb C) =\bigoplus\_{p+q=m}H^{p,q}(X). \\]

| Existing RTG Hodge theory           | Algebraic-geometric Hodge theory                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Cochains on a discrete complex      | Differential forms on a complex manifold              |
| Exact, coexact, harmonic sectors    | Holomorphic and antiholomorphic bidegrees \\((p,q)\\) |
| Combinatorial Laplacian             | Complex geometry, Kähler structure and harmonic forms |
| Betti numbers and harmonic topology | Rational \\((k,k)\\) classes and algebraic cycles     |

Both are legitimate mathematical uses of Hodge theory, but the first does not establish the second.

We need a bridge through an actual complex projective variety. The dyadic structure offers one.

## 2. A projective algebraic curve from the RTG dyadic operator

[SESSION — ALG: independently checked exact matrix identities; input from Dyadic v6.4.19 §§6.1–6.3 and Genesis v2.8.9 §6.6]

Recall

\\[ A= \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad J_Q=\frac{2A-I}{\sqrt3}, \qquad J_Q^2=-I. \\]

The B-metric is

\\[ B= \begin{pmatrix} 1&-\frac12\\\ -\frac12&1 \end{pmatrix}. \\]

Both identities hold exactly:

\\[ A^\top BA=B,\qquad J_Q^\top BJ_Q=B. \\]

Now introduce the complex coordinate

\\[ z=x+\zeta_3 y,\qquad \zeta_3=e^{2\pi i/3}. \\]

In this coordinate system, \\(J_Q\\) acts as multiplication by \\(i\\), and \\(A\\) acts as multiplication by \\(\zeta_6=e^{i\pi/3}\\).

Choose the integer lattice

\\[ \Lambda=\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\zeta_3 =\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\zeta_6. \\]

Because \\(A\\) has integer entries and determinant one, it preserves the corresponding rank-two lattice exactly.

We can therefore construct

\\[ \boxed{ E\_{\rm RTG}:=\mathbb C/\Lambda. } \\]

### The critical extra fact: an integral Kähler form

Define

\\[ \omega_B(v,w)=B(J_Qv,w). \\]

The exact matrix calculation gives

\\[ J_Q^\top B= \frac{\sqrt3}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\\\-1&0 \end{pmatrix}. \\]

After normalization,

\\[ \boxed{ \omega_0=\frac2{\sqrt3}\omega_B=dx\wedge dy. } \\]

On the chosen torus,

\\[ \int\_{E\_{\rm RTG}}\omega_0=1. \\]

Thus \\([\omega_0]\\) is an integral cohomology class and is represented by the first Chern class of a degree-one line bundle.

[SESSION — ALG] We have a positive integral polarization compatible with the RTG-derived complex structure. It supplies the projective-algebraic realization needed for Hodge theory.

One crucial qualification: the integer lattice is a mathematical choice, not a currently certified primitive RTG physical lattice. Nor has the full nonlinear dyadic map been shown to descend to this elliptic curve.

## 3. A complete Hodge-class calculation on an RTG-derived algebraic surface

[SESSION — ALG: new calculation using the RTG-derived \\(A\\) and standard intersection theory]

Take two copies of the elliptic curve:

\\[ \boxed{X=E\_{\rm RTG}\times E\_{\rm RTG}.} \\]

This is a smooth projective algebraic surface of complex dimension two.

Its second cohomology has Hodge decomposition

\\[ H^2(X,\mathbb C) =H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X), \\]

with

\\[ h^{2,0}=1,\quad h^{1,1}=4,\quad h^{0,2}=1. \\]

We now seek four independent algebraic cycles whose classes span \\(H^{1,1}(X)\\).

### Four cycles naturally associated with the dyadic structure

Let \\(O\\) be the identity of the elliptic curve, and let \\(a:E\to E\\) be the automorphism induced by the integer matrix \\(A\\).

Define four algebraic curves in \\(X\\):

\\[ \begin{aligned} D_1&=\\{O\\}\times E,\\\ D_2&=E\times\\{O\\},\\\ D_3&=\\{(z,z):z\in E\\},\\\ D_4&=\\{(z,a(z)):z\in E\\}. \end{aligned} \\]

The first two are coordinate fibers; the third is the diagonal; the fourth is the graph of the dyadic sixfold rotation.

All four are algebraic curves because they are fibers, a diagonal, or the graph of an algebraic elliptic-curve automorphism.

Their intersection numbers form the matrix

\\[ \boxed{ M= \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&1&1\\\ 1&0&1&1\\\ 1&1&0&1\\\ 1&1&1&0 \end{pmatrix}. } \\]

The entries are exact. For example, the diagonal and graph intersect at fixed points of \\(a\\), and

\\[ \\#\ker(a-I)=|\det(A-I)|=1. \\]

Each curve has self-intersection zero, and the remaining distinct pairs intersect once.

I independently checked

\\[ \boxed{\det M=-3\ne0.} \\]

Therefore the four divisor classes are linearly independent in \\(H^2(X,\mathbb Q)\\). Since \\(h^{1,1}(X)=4\\), they span \\(H^{1,1}(X)\\) over \\(\mathbb C\\).

And because these four classes are rational, we obtain

\\[ \boxed{ H^{1,1}(X)\cap H^2(X,\mathbb Q) = \operatorname{span}\_{\mathbb Q} \\{[D_1],[D_2],[D_3],[D_4]\\}. } \\]

Every rational Hodge class of codimension one on this surface is therefore explicitly represented by a rational linear combination of algebraic cycles.

### What have we established?

For \\(k=1\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Im}\bigl(\operatorname{cl}\_1\bigr) =H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb Q). } \\]

For \\(k=0\\), the class is generated by the variety itself. For \\(k=2\\), \\(H^{2,2}(X,\mathbb Q)\\) is generated by the class of a point.

Hence the rational Hodge Conjecture holds for every codimension on this explicitly constructed surface.

This calculation works. Its novelty relative to RTG is the transparent connection between the dyadic matrix \\(A\\), Eisenstein multiplication, and a concrete basis of algebraic cycles. The underlying Hodge statement is already known from the classical Lefschetz \\((1,1)\\) theorem and the Hodge Conjecture for surfaces.&#x20;

## 4. A limitation: the full nonlinear RTG dynamics is not a holomorphic elliptic-curve automorphism

The construction above uses the linearized dyadic symmetry. Can we identify the full nonlinear map \\(T\\) with an algebraic automorphism of the elliptic curve?

The answer is negative under the natural holomorphic-realization contract.

[OWNER: Dyadic v6.4.19 §6.14, `DYAD-ALG-NF6`]

The six-step return has a nonzero cubic term:

\\[ T^6\_{\rm NF}(z) =z+\mu z^2\bar z+O(|z|^5), \qquad \mu=-\frac12-\frac{2i\sqrt3}{3}\ne0. \\]

[SESSION — ALG: holomorphic elliptic-curve realization obstruction]

Every holomorphic self-map of an elliptic curve fixing its identity lifts to a complex-linear map \\(z\mapsto az\\) preserving its lattice.

If the derivative at the fixed point is \\(\zeta_6\\), the map must be multiplication by \\(\zeta_6\\). Its sixth iterate is therefore the identity.

But \\(T^6\ne\mathrm{id}\\), because its cubic return coefficient is nonzero.

Consequently,

\\[ \boxed{ T\text{ cannot be locally conjugate to a holomorphic } E\_{\rm RTG}\text{-endomorphism fixing the origin.} } \\]

This does not exclude every possible algebraic-geometric model of RTG. It establishes that the nonlinear dyadic dynamics cannot simply be identified with the holomorphic automorphism of the elliptic curve constructed from its linearization.

This matters because it prevents us from silently transporting dynamical RTG claims into algebraic geometry.

## 5. Can we enter the genuinely difficult part of the Hodge Conjecture?

The interesting problem begins when we move beyond divisors and ask about rational \\((2,2)\\) classes on complex fourfolds.

Consider

\\[ X_4=E\_{\rm RTG}^{\\,4}. \\]

This is a smooth projective fourfold. We can calculate

\\[ H^4(X_4,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{2,2}(X_4) \\]

using the exterior algebra of its first cohomology, with the complex structure induced by four copies of \\(J_Q\\).

The calculation is finite-dimensional and amenable to exact rational linear algebra.

However, products of elliptic curves belong to an extensively studied class for which the rational Hodge Conjecture is already known. Computing their classes would be a valuable RTG-to-algebraic-geometry verification, not a solution to an unresolved case.

The genuinely challenging extension would involve an RTG-derived complex variety with Hodge classes whose algebraicity is not already guaranteed by familiar divisor-generation results.

That requires additional structures not yet established by RTG: higher-dimensional integrable complex geometry, suitable integral polarizations, and a mechanism for constructing algebraic cycles representing the relevant rational cohomology classes.

## 7. The main mathematical insight

The chain we have actually established is

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} A^6=I,\quad A^\top BA=B\\\\[3pt] \Downarrow\\\\[3pt] J_Q^2=-I\\\\[3pt] \Downarrow\quad\text{chosen integral lattice}\\\\[3pt] E=\mathbb C/\mathbb Z[\zeta_6]\\\\[3pt] \Downarrow\quad\text{integral polarization}\\\\[3pt] \text{Smooth projective elliptic curve}\\\\[3pt] \Downarrow\\\\[3pt] \text{Hodge decomposition and algebraic cycles}. \end{gathered} } \\]

The arrow requiring the chosen integral lattice is especially important. Without it, the real complex-structure operator \\(J_Q\\) does not establish a rational cohomology lattice, and without a suitable higher-dimensional polarization, a complex torus need not be projective.

These are precisely the kinds of extra mathematical structures that matter to the Hodge Conjecture.

### My assessment

There is enough here to justify a focused RTG study, but I would frame it as RTG Complex Geometry and Hodge Realization, not as a claimed approach to solving the Hodge Conjecture.

The first concrete result would be the four-cycle spanning calculation above, paired with the holomorphic-dynamics obstruction. Together they establish both what the dyadic structure genuinely supplies and what it does not.

The central open question would then become:

Can a physically selected RTG relational mechanism determine an integral, polarized complex geometry in which nontrivial higher-codimensional rational Hodge classes admit an explicit algebraic-cycle construction?

That is a mathematically precise research target. It also offers immediate falsifiers: failure of integrability, failure of polarization, lack of a physical lattice selection, or a Hodge class outside the span of the proposed cycles.

None of those issues is resolved by \\(i^2=-1\\) alone—but the dyadic construction gives us a rigorous starting object on which to investigate them.
