# HCG — Degree 26: a two-block Galois/reflection obstruction

**Date:** 30 October 2026  
**Provenance:** New SESSION derivation by ChatGPT; **PROPOSED — independent peer proof review required**. INTERNAL.  
**Not a new adopted lower bound.** The current independently checked lower bound remains d>=26 until this argument is separately checked.

## 0. Target and inherited hypotheses

Work in the existing (H) nonsplit Weil-eightfold feasibility class over K=Q(sqrt(-3)). In particular:

- Gamma <= GL_d(K) is a faithful absolutely irreducible finite representation with character field **exactly K**, arising from a connected finite cover of P1.
- The multiplicity variation has K-dimension 8, balanced signature (4,4), and the required period-map rank is 16.
- Only effective branch points are counted. Their product-one tuple generates Gamma, N>=19, and sum c(g_j)=2d+8.
- The already reviewed reflection dichotomy implies Gamma is reflection-free in the nonsplit dominant case, hence c(g_j)>=2.
- The already reviewed complex-imprimitive period factorization gives inducing rank r>=6 and genus gamma=0 for the intermediate curve.
- For K-defined blocks, the prior Hodge-Shapiro/discriminant argument requires degree >=50 in a nonsplit dominant datum. Therefore every block system relevant below is non-K-defined.
- The prior primitive exclusion through degree 42 and the tensor/tensor-induced budgets exclude other degree-26 branches.

The purpose is to examine the **only** remaining degree-26 case, not assume that a group/family already exists.

## 1. Budget equality forces exactly two rank-13 involutions

A transitive block system has d=kr, r>=6 and r+2k>=17. At d=26 this forces k=2,r=13. Let Lambda be the index-two block stabilizer (the kernel of the permutation action on the two blocks). For the intermediate double cover Y=C/Lambda -> P1, genus zero and Riemann–Hurwitz give

    sum_j ind(pi_j)=2.

Thus exactly two local branch monodromies swap the blocks. On an element swapping two r-dimensional blocks,

    c(g)=r+rank(1-P_g),

where P_g is its return map on a block. Each of the other N-2 effective local matrices has moving rank at least 2. Consequently

    60=sum c_j >= 26+2(N-2) >= 60.

Every inequality is an equality. Therefore

    N=19;   (c_j)=(13,13,2^17);                     (1)

and each swapping element has P_g=1. In block matrix form it is

    [[0,A],[A^{-1},0]],

so its square is exactly the identity. No semisimple-power or central-scalar ambiguity remains in this representation.

## 2. Non-K-defined blocks must be Galois conjugate

Write V_C=U_1 direct-sum U_2, with dim U_i=13 and Lambda fixing both. The restrictions tau_i on U_i are irreducible: a proper Lambda-submodule of one block together with its translate would give a proper Gamma-submodule.

The two Lambda-characters are **distinct**. By Clifford theory for a cyclic index-two extension, an invariant irreducible Lambda-character extends over C; inducing it would then split, contrary to irreducibility of V_C. Equivalently, the inertia group of tau_1 is exactly Lambda.

Because every Gamma matrix is K-defined, Gal(Kbar/K) permutes the two irreducible Lambda constituents. If both constituents were fixed individually, their central primitive idempotents in Kbar[Lambda] would have coefficients in K and their images would be K-defined blocks. That is the already excluded K-imprimitive case.

Hence some alpha in Gal(Kbar/K) exchanges tau_1 and tau_2. For every h in Lambda,

    rank(1-tau_1(h))=rank(1-tau_2(h)),              (2)

since Galois automorphisms preserve the multiplicity of eigenvalue 1. This applies to each of the seventeen block-preserving branch generators in (1). Their two ranks add to 2, so they are **(1,1)**: each acts as a complex reflection on both blocks.

This step does NOT assume that arbitrary complex block systems are Galois stable. Here the blocks are precisely the two distinct isotypic constituents for the K-defined normal subgroup Lambda, which makes their unordered pair Galois stable.

## 3. The inducing image is a rank-13 reflection group

The intermediate curve Y is P1. Above the two swapping branch points, local inertia for C->Y is generated by the square of the swapping element; it is trivial by §1.

Each of the other seventeen base branch points has two preimages in Y. In the tau_1 local system, each preimage has nontrivial rank-one local monodromy: one is tau_1(g_j), the other is its translate corresponding to tau_2(g_j). Thus the effective tau_1 local system on Y has **34 reflection punctures**, and no further nontrivial punctures.

Its monodromy group R=tau_1(Lambda) is generated by these local elements, because a sphere punctured at these points has its fundamental group generated by their meridians. It is finite and irreducible of dimension 13. Therefore R is an irreducible finite complex reflection group of rank 13.

This is a reflection-generation argument, not the invalid implication that a group containing some reflections must be generated by them.

## 4. Shephard–Todd leaves two alternatives; both fail

For rank 13 the classification gives only:

1. an irreducible monomial reflection group G(m,p,13), or
2. the symmetric group S_14 in its standard rank-13 reflection representation.

The exceptional irreducible reflection groups have rank at most 8. Shephard–Todd (1954), §§2–3, proves the imprimitive groups are monomial and lists the primitive groups; the official Magma catalogue gives the same high-rank consequence.

### Monomial alternative

If tau_1 is monomial, tau_1=Ind_J^Lambda chi for a finite rank-one character chi. Transitivity of induction gives

    sigma_C=Ind_Lambda^Gamma(tau_1)=Ind_J^Gamma chi.

Thus sigma is complex-monomial. The already checked field-independent rank-one period bound is at most 12, below 16. No K-defined line system is needed for this contradiction.

### Standard symmetric alternative

The standard S_14 character is integer-valued (number of fixed letters minus 1). Therefore chi_{tau_1}:Lambda->Q. Induction from an index-two normal subgroup gives

    chi_sigma(h)=chi_{tau_1}(h)+chi_{tau_1}(s^{-1}hs) in Q  (h in Lambda),
    chi_sigma(g)=0                                           (g not in Lambda).

The full character field is consequently Q, contrary to the hypothesis that it is exactly K.

One can also close this alternative directly at §2: a rational irreducible constituent has its isotypic projector over K, so this block system descends to K. The character argument above avoids any separate Schur-index assumption.

## 5. Proposed conclusion and nonclaims

Under the inherited hypotheses, **the remaining non-K-defined two-block degree-26 branch is impossible**. Combined with the already checked degree-27 exclusion, peer acceptance of this note would improve the necessary bound from d>=26 to d>=28.

This is a new proof proposal, not a finite tuple search, not a statement that a degree-28 family exists, and not a general theorem about all finite imprimitive groups. It uses period/dimension results specific to the HCG feasibility problem.

No nonsplit discriminant is transported across a non-K-defined block. The proof uses that block only to establish reflection generation and then contradicts either period rank or the exact character field.

## 6. Peer-review priorities

The critical points are: (i) the equality budget and involutivity at the two ramified points; (ii) the multiplicity-free index-two Clifford decomposition and its Galois action; (iii) reflection generation of the inducing image on genus-zero Y; (iv) the two rank-13 classification alternatives. A failure of any of these would leave degree 26 OPEN; this note must not be recorded as CHECKED merely from numeric budget tests.
