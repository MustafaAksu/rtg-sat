## Decision

**Search \(d=6\) first, and begin with generating \(G_{34}\) monodromy tuples.** Two exact local-rank patterns survive the dimension budget: (20 rank-one reflections) and (18 rank-one reflections plus one rank-two element at \(N=19\)). Both necessarily generate a **finite complex reflection-group image**. Consequently, looking for groups *not generated by reflections* is unnecessary in dimension six. The Shephard–Todd classification plus Severn's monomial exclusion and the requirement that the character field be exactly \(K\) singles out \(G_{34}\) as the first non-monomial, non-real-reflection candidate.

**Priority A:** \(G_{34}\), 20 involutive reflection branch cycles, product 1 and generating the group; inspect Nielsen/Hurwitz classes beyond the known **paired split** class and compute exact Hermitian discriminants. For involutive local monodromies, Severn v2 Theorem 5.1 supplies (L) under its stated inverse-conjugacy premise; in this case that premise is automatic. Chevalley–Weil gives signature (4,4). **Neither nonsplit occurrence nor (D) is established.**

**Priority B:** \(G_{34}\), 19 branch cycles (18 involutive rank-one reflections plus one rank-two element). First enumerate the rank-two conjugacy classes that can be the inverse of a product of 18 reflections; check whether the local inverse-conjugacy condition holds or supplier-lattice verification is necessary. Compute discriminant before any interior period-map Jacobian.

**Priority C:** \(d=7,8\) irreducible, character-field-\(K\) representations with *almost* reflection-generated local monodromy. The total excess of local fixed-space codimensions over 1 is bounded by \(2d-11\) (3 at \(d=7\), 5 at \(d=8\)). Search only finite-group classes having sufficiently many rank-one/rank-two elements, not arbitrary high-degree primitive groups. Higher degrees follow only if these screens fail.

## I. Rigorous branch budget and reflection-generated image

Let \(d=\dim_K\sigma\), \(N\) be the number of **effective** branch cycles, and \(c_i=\operatorname{rank}_K(1-\sigma(g_i))\ge1\). Assume irreducibility, no invariants, product one in the image, and \(\dim_K H^1_{\rm par}=8\). Standard parabolic dimension and dominance give

\[
\sum_i c_i=2d+8,\qquad N\ge 16+3=19,
\qquad \varepsilon:=\sum_i(c_i-1)=2d+8-N\le 2d-11.
\]

For \(d=6\), \(\varepsilon\le1\). If \(N=20\), all locals are rank-one reflections. If \(N=19\), precisely one local has rank 2 and all remaining locals are reflections. In the latter case product one forces the rank-two matrix to equal the inverse of the product of the 18 reflection matrices. Therefore the entire image \(\sigma(G)\) is generated by the reflections. This does **not** imply that the original \(G\) equals its image when \(\sigma\) has kernel; supplier computations may still depend on original \(G\).

For an image generated by order-two reflections, \(N=20\) gives signature (4,4) by the Chevalley–Weil multiplicity rule \(-6+20/2=4\), at both conjugate embeddings. At \(N=19\), if the 18 rank-one reflections have order 2, product one forces the remaining rank-two element to have determinant 1. Its two nontrivial unitary eigenvalues are reciprocal, contributing total fractional age 1; \(-6+18/2+1=4\). These are **conditional**, not general claims about arbitrary complex reflection orders.

| \(d\) | Allowed maximum excess \(\varepsilon\) | First search consequence |
|---:|---:|---|
| 6 | 1 | Reflection-generated image necessarily; focus \(G_{34}\) |
| 7 | 3 | At most three excess ranks; non-reflection image requires at least two non-reflection locals |
| 8 | 5 | Same near-reflection search principle |
| 9 | 7 | More possibilities; defer until smaller degrees assessed |

### Additional simple scalar-extension exclusion (SESSION lemma, peer review pending)

Let \(G=\langle W,\omega I_d\rangle\) with \(W\subset GL_d(\mathbb R)\) finite, and suppose the local monodromies generate \(G\) and multiply to one. Any element \(\omega^a w\) with \(a\not\equiv0\pmod3\) and \(w\in W\) has fixed-space codimension at least \(\lceil d/2\rceil\): the eigenvalues \(\omega^{-a}\) and \(\omega^a\) of the real matrix \(w\) occur with equal multiplicity. At least two local elements must lie outside \(W\), because their product has trivial quotient class. Hence

\[
\varepsilon\ge 2(\lceil d/2\rceil-1).
\]

This exceeds \(2d-11\) for \(d=6,7,8,9\), ruling out **these specific scalar extensions of real groups** in the stated genus-zero rank-16 transfer budget. It does **not** rule out general non-real finite groups or all complex character-field extensions.

## II. Explicit smaller intermediate covers of the G34 W5 Galois cover

The already archived independent W5 auditor certificate has an orbit of **756 Eisenstein vectors** and 10 generating reflection permutations (each of order two). We extracted only `round1/outputs/finite_root_orbit.json` without executing any archive code. `g34_intermediate_cover_probe.py` independently verifies the following exact counts:

| Group action / intermediate cover | Degree | Each local involution: fixed points | Each local involution: 2-cycles | Genus for W5's 20 paired branch cycles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| G34 action on 756 vectors | **756** | **270** | **243** | **1675** |
| G34 action on 126 projective root lines | 126 | 46 | 40 | 275 |
| Full G34 regular cover (group order 39,191,040) | 39,191,040 | 0 | 19,595,520 | 156,764,161 |

Riemann–Hurwitz for the 756-sheet cover: \(2g-2=-2(756)+20(243)=3348\), hence **\(g=1675\)**. The 756-vector action is transitive. The stabilizer of a vector has index 756, hence order \(39,191,040/756=51,840\). This subgroup **fixes a nonzero vector in the defining six-dimensional representation**, so Frobenius reciprocity ensures that the representation occurs in \(K[G/H]\). Thus the intermediate cover is a genuine possible carrier of the required isotypic component (not yet an explicit equation or an interior-period solution).

**Negative control:** The 126-line action is more economical but the standard central scalar \(\omega I_6\in G_{34}\) acts trivially on lines and nontrivially on the defining representation. Consequently that six-dimensional representation cannot occur in \(K[\text{126 lines}]\). The script verifies scalar compatibility and centrality of the induced order-three scalar permutation, while membership of \(\omega I_6\) in \(G_{34}\) relies on the group description in the reflection-group classification. **Do not use the 126-sheet quotient as a surrogate for the target six-dimensional isotypic part.**

The degree-756 cover is *dramatically smaller* than the full Galois cover, rebutting a literal claim that every calculation must use genus about \(1.6\times10^8\). It is nevertheless genus 1675, and no explicit \(F^1\) or exact 16-by-16 interior period Jacobian has been produced. The genus calculation is for the existing paired **split** W5 branch class; identical local cycle types give the same genus for other G34 reflection tuples if their 756-vector action is connected.
