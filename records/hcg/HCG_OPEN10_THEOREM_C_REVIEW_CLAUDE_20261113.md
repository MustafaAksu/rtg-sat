## 1. Lemma 11.1 — the invariants of SL(W) in Λ

**Claim.** Λ^{SL(W)} is generated, as an algebra, by the θ(u, u′), the det(W ⊗ a) and the det(W^* ⊗ a′).

| Step | Content | Check |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | First fundamental theorem for SL(V), dim V = N, characteristic 0: the polynomial invariants of p vectors and q covectors are generated by the scalar products and by the determinants of N vectors and of N covectors | **import** (Kraft–Procesi §8.4; classical, Weyl). Not re-read |
| L2 | An SL(W)-invariant element of W^{⊗p} ⊗ W^{*⊗q} is an invariant polynomial on (W^*)^p ⊕ W^q that is homogeneous of degree one in each of the p + q variables | Duality between tensors and multilinear functions; apply L1 with V = W^*. ✓ |
| L3 | A polynomial in the generators of L1 that is of degree one in each variable is a linear combination of products of generators in which every variable occurs exactly once | Each generator is multilinear in the variables it involves, so the component of multidegree (1, …, 1) of any polynomial in the generators consists of such products. ✓ |
| L4 | So the invariants of W^{⊗p} ⊗ W^{*⊗q} are spanned by tensor products of three kinds of elementary invariants on disjoint sets of factors: the contraction Σ w_i ⊗ w_i^* (one factor W, one factor W^*); the determinant tensor in N factors W; the dual determinant tensor in N factors W^* | Scalar product ↔ contraction; determinant of N variables in W^* ↔ the alternating tensor in W^{⊗N}. ✓ |
| L5 | ∧^p(W ⊗ U) ⊗ ∧^q(W^* ⊗ U′) is the image of W^{⊗p} ⊗ W^{*⊗q} ⊗ U^{⊗p} ⊗ U′^{⊗q} under antisymmetrization, which is SL(W)-equivariant and surjective. SL(W) is reductive, so the invariants of the image are the image of the invariants | Complete reducibility. SL(W) acts trivially on the U-factors, so the invariants upstairs are (invariants of L4) ⊗ U^{⊗p} ⊗ U′^{⊗q}. ✓ |
| L6 | Antisymmetrization turns a tensor product of blocks into the product in Λ of the images of the blocks, up to sign | After a permutation of the tensor positions, which antisymmetrization absorbs with a sign. ✓ |
| L7 | The image of a contraction block with the factors u_k, u′_l is θ(u_k, u′_l) | Direct. ✓ |
| L8 | The image of a determinant block with factors u_{k_1}, …, u_{k_N} is Σ_π sgn(π)(w_{π(1)} ⊗ u_{k_1}) ∧ … ∧ (w_{π(N)} ⊗ u_{k_N}), which is the full polarization of a ↦ N!·det(W ⊗ a) | **Recomputed:** reordering the wedge factors so that the w's are in order costs sgn(π), so the sum is Σ_π (w_1 ⊗ u_{k_{π⁻¹(1)}}) ∧ … ∧ (w_N ⊗ u_{k_{π⁻¹(N)}}), which is N! times the polarization of the degree-N map a ↦ (w_1 ⊗ a) ∧ … ∧ (w_N ⊗ a). ✓ |
| L9 | By the polarization identity this is a linear combination of values det(W ⊗ a); the same for the dual blocks | The polarization of a homogeneous polynomial map is a combination of its values at sums of the arguments. ✓ |

**Result: PASS**, given the import L1. The lemma is the multilinear, skew form of the first fundamental theorem.

## 2. Theorem 11.2 — from Weil classes to all powers

**Claim.** Let (A, ι, E) be a polarized abelian 2n-fold of Weil type for K, n ≥ 2, whose Weil classes are algebraic and whose Hodge group is SU_H. Then every Hodge class on every power A^m is algebraic.

| Step | Content | Check |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | H¹(A, ℂ) = W ⊕ W′, the two eigenspaces of K, each of dimension N = 2n. The polarization identifies W′ with W^* as representations of SU_H(ℂ) = SL(W) | E pairs the two eigenspaces (E(ι(a)x, y) = E(x, ι(ā)y)) and is invariant under the Hodge group. ✓ |
| T2 | The Hodge group of A^m is SU_H acting diagonally | Hg(A^m) = Hg(A): standard. ✓ |
| T3 | So the Hodge classes on A^m, tensored with ℂ, are Λ^{SL(W)} with U = U′ = ℂ^m | H¹(A^m) = H¹(A) ⊗ ℚ^m, so the cohomology algebra is ∧(W ⊗ U) ⊗ ∧(W^* ⊗ U). Hodge classes are the invariants of the Hodge group, and invariants commute with extension of scalars. ✓ |
| T4 | The θ(u, u′) lie in degree two, so they are complex combinations of rational Hodge classes of degree two, hence of divisor classes | Lefschetz (1,1) theorem. ✓ |
| T5 | With 𝒪 the order of a ∈ K with ι(a) ∈ End(A): for a ∈ 𝒪^m the homomorphism A^m → A, (x_i) ↦ Σ ι(a_i)x_i pulls the two Weil lines of A back to det(W ⊗ ã) and det(W^* ⊗ conj(ã)), ã the image of a under the fixed embedding | The pull-back on H¹ is w ↦ w ⊗ ã on W and w′ ↦ w′ ⊗ conj(ã) on W′, and W_K(A) ⊗ ℂ = ∧^N W ⊕ ∧^N W′. These classes are pull-backs of algebraic classes. ✓ |
| T6 | The ã, a ∈ 𝒪^m, are Zariski dense in U, so the classes det(W ⊗ ã) span all det(W ⊗ a); likewise for W^* | The image of 𝒪 is an infinite subset of ℂ, hence Zariski dense, and so is its m-th power. A linear form vanishing on the values of the polynomial map a ↦ det(W ⊗ a) on a dense set vanishes on all values. The conjugates conj(ã) are dense too. ✓ |
| T7 | By Lemma 11.1 the products of these classes span all Hodge classes over ℂ; a rational subspace contained in the complex span of algebraic classes lies in their rational span | The descent is the last sentence of the proof of v2 Cor. 4.7 (elementary linear algebra). ✓ |

**Result: PASS**, given Lemma 11.1.

**Remarks.**
- The proof uses the hypothesis Hg(A) = SU_H in T2–T3 and nowhere else; it uses nothing about K beyond being imaginary quadratic, and nothing about the discriminant.
- **The case m = 1** gives: if Hg(A) = SU_H, the Hodge ring of A is generated by divisor classes and the Weil classes. So statement S2 of `HCG_HODGE_SCOPE_NOTE_CLAUDE_20261112.md`, recorded there as a classical import not re-read, also follows from Lemma 11.1 as reviewed here (still on the import L1).
- The hypothesis n ≥ 2 is not used visibly in the proof; it does no harm.

## 4. §12 — abelian sixfolds of type III (a reading, not a full review)

**Corollary 12.1** (paraphrase). Let D be a totally definite quaternion algebra over ℚ containing ℚ(√−3) (ramified at ∞ and at an odd number of primes, each 3 or ≡ 2 mod 3), and 𝒜 a PEL family of abelian sixfolds for D whose general member has endomorphism algebra D. For every member whose Hodge group is the generic Hodge group of the family, every Hodge class on every power is algebraic.

| Step | Content | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | K = ℚ(√−3) embeds in D as a maximal subfield | The ramified primes of D are 3 (ramified in K) or ≡ 2 mod 3 (inert in K), so none splits in K; this is the standard embedding criterion. Cited to Voight. ✓ as a standard fact |
| Q2 | Restricting the action to K makes A a sixfold of Weil type for K with a compatible polarization | An element of D anticommuting with √−3 exchanges the two eigenspaces on H^{1,0}, so the multiplicities are (3, 3). The compatibility of the polarization with the canonical involution of D is cited to Abdulali §4. ✓ for the multiplicities; the polarization statement **as cited** |
| Q3 | By Theorem B the Weil classes for K are algebraic | Uses the every-member scope of Theorem B: a type III member is a special member of the Weil moduli space. That scope is the subject of OPEN 8 (closed in scope by the PI on 13 Nov 2026) |
| Q4 | Abdulali's Theorem 4.1: for a general member of an effective PEL family of type III, the Hodge ring is generated by divisors and the Weil cycles of a maximal subfield of the endomorphism algebra, and the algebraicity of those Weil cycles gives the Hodge conjecture for all powers | **import, not read.** Severn quotes the statement |
| Q5 | Severn's extension: "general" in Abdulali means that the Hodge group is the generic one, and the proof there computes invariants of that group only, so it applies to every member with that Hodge group; and the maximal subfield may be taken to be K | **Cannot be checked without Abdulali's paper.** It is a claim about what that proof uses |

**Result: NOT REVIEWED beyond Q1–Q3.** Corollary 12.1 stands or falls with Q4–Q5, which need the source.

**Example 12.2** (non-split discriminants are reached by type III sixfolds). The arithmetic was recomputed exactly (`evidence/hcg/verification/severn_v2_example_12_2_check.py`): for D = (−3, −1)_ℚ, nrd(αu + βv + γuv) = 3α² + β² + 3γ²; the Gram matrix of the K-component in the basis 1, v and its determinant −b·nrd(t); the products 45 and 396. Hence the classes [−5] and [−11], and 5 and 11 are inert in K, so neither is a norm: both are non-split. ✓. **Not checked:** that the two displayed forms satisfy the positivity conditions of a polarization, and the general formula for the discriminant of a type III sixfold beyond this computation.
