## 3. Why this is not yet a C6-cover check

For a connected cyclic C6 cover, a rank-one character has
\[
m(\chi_a)=-2+\#\{j: a\,e_j\not\equiv0\pmod6\},
\]
with nonzero branch exponents `e_j in {1,...,5}`. The present `M` has rational character multiplicities `m(chi_1)=8`, `m(chi_4)=1`, `m(chi_3)=0`. If it came from such a cover, m(chi_1)=8 would require ten effective branch points; m(chi_3)=0 would require exactly **two odd exponents**, hence eight even exponents (2 or 4). Then m(chi_4) >= -2+8 = 6, contradicting m(chi_4)=1. Therefore the constructed module is **not a candidate cyclic-cover cohomology profile**; it must not be marketed as the requested real-curve C6 test.

For a **genuine** C6 example the next task remains: construct a concrete Jacobian `H^1(C,Z_2)` with its deck action and integral cup form for a *source-registered* branch tuple (e.g. the rank-eight separator), compute saturated L and L' and their `S`-constituents. The existing `cyclic_disc_claude.py` evaluates a rational **parabolic-cup determinant**, not the saturated Jacobian lattice, so it alone cannot complete that task.
