## 2. Simultaneous realization of the 17 operators

### Boundary and component

**[THM — independent construction]** W5’s 17 interval curves form a laminar pants decomposition. Pinching them produces a stable twenty-pointed rational curve with **18 trivalent components and 17 nodes**.

Every W5 boundary product is the identity. Consequently, the cover has trivial inertia at every node. Its normalization consists of ten component types with monodromy triples
\[
(s_i,s_i,1)
\]
and eight with triples
\[
(1,1,1).
\]

Start with the connected marked \(G\)-cover specified by W5, cut along the lifted curves, and glue using the **sheet identifications inherited from that cover**. This last condition is essential: arbitrary gluing would not establish membership in the required component.

All node stabilizers are trivial. The admissible-cover deformation relation \(q_j=t_j^{e_j}\) therefore has \(e_j=1\) throughout. The 17 base smoothing parameters are independent. Smoothing reverses the original cut, establishing that the degeneration lies in \(\overline{S_{\mathrm{W5}}}\). The deformation framework is supported by Romagny–Wewers, Theorem 5.6, and Abramovich–Corti–Vistoli’s twisted/admissible-cover results. [Emis](https://emis.de/ft/47206)

Some upstairs rational components become unstable after forgetting ramification markings. The argument does **not** assume that all 17 parameters remain independent after stabilizing the upstairs curve. Independence is required on the marked-base/admissible-cover chart.

### Coefficient transport

**[THM — VERIFIED]** Dettweiler–Wewers’ formula includes both the braid action and coefficient transport. Omitting the latter without justification would leave a gap. [Emis](https://emis.de/ft/47210)

Here the coefficient system comes from an actual finite \(G\)-cover. For an exact tuple-return loop, its transport element \(h\) satisfies
\[
hg_i h^{-1}=g_i\qquad\forall i.
\]
Since the \(g_i\) generate \(G\), \(h\in Z(G)\). Absolute irreducibility makes its representation scalar.

The important distinction is
\[
\text{Schur’s lemma}\Rightarrow\text{scalar},
\qquad
h\in G,\ |G|<\infty\Rightarrow\text{finite order}.
\]
Irreducibility alone would not establish finite order.

For the constructed degeneration, choose an exterior unramified section. Its inverse image is a finite étale \(G\)-torsor over the whole contractible polydisc, so it admits a sheet section. This pointing removes the coefficient ambiguity simultaneously.

Without that pointing, the scalar lies in \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), hence in \(\mu_6(K)\). A common sixth-power cover removes it.

Thus, in one common fibre identification, the geometric logarithms have the form
\[
\boxed{
M_j=\varepsilon c_j\,A N_jA^{-1},
\qquad
c_j\in\mathbb Z_{>0},
\quad
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\text{ independent of }j,
}
\]
with **one common** change of basis \(A\), not independently chosen conjugations.

### Plumbing orientation and infinity

**[THM — VERIFIED]** In \(xy=q\), write \(q=re^{i\theta}\). A collar trivialization is
\[
x_\theta=e^{i\theta\chi}x_0,\qquad
y_\theta=e^{i\theta(1-\chi)}y_0,
\]
where the cutoff \(\chi\) interpolates between the two boundary circles. At \(\theta=2\pi\), both boundaries are fixed and the annulus undergoes one full twist.

Using this construction at every holomorphic node gives the same direction. Exchanging \(x,y\), multiplying \(q\) by a holomorphic unit, or replacing \(q\) by \(t^c\), \(c>0\), does not introduce an independent sign. Comparison with the cocycle convention may reverse **all** twists together.

The infinity convention can be fixed by keeping all twenty branch points finite and using the exterior unramified section. Dettweiler–Wewers distinguishes this from placing a branch point at infinity; the latter requires its own base-point convention. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310139)

Accordingly, W5’s \([19,20]\) and \([1,18]\) twists induce **equal operators on parabolic cohomology**, not inverse operators. This equality is also checked exactly in the delivered script.

## 3. Polarized VHS and the nilpotent-orbit step

### The VHS is geometric, not postulated

**[THM — VERIFIED]** The smooth projective \(G\)-cover supplies a polarized weight-one variation through \(H^1\). Taking the rational isotypic component associated with the conjugate representation pair and a primitive matrix factor gives a rational polarized variation of
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_{\mathbb Q}=16,\qquad \dim_KV=8.
\]

The factor can be obtained using the corresponding rational central idempotent and the rank-one reflection idempotent \((1-s_1)/2\). Clearing denominators realizes the image as an abelian factor up to isogeny. This construction does not use the companion transfer theorem for algebraic cycles.

The two \(K\)-embeddings have Hodge dimensions \((4,4)\), giving a period domain of complex dimension \(16\).

### Hypotheses needed for Schmid

**[THM — VERIFIED]** At this point there is an actual polarized variation over \((\Delta^*)^{17}\), with commuting unipotent monodromies, a stable rational/integral structure, a horizontal period map and flat \(K\)-action. These are geometric hypotheses; arbitrary matrices satisfying H0–H4 would not supply them.

Schmid’s several-variable theorem (4.12) gives
\[
\widetilde\Phi(z)
=
\exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{17}z_jM_j\right)\Psi(t),
\qquad t_j=e^{2\pi iz_j},
\]
where \(\Psi\) extends holomorphically to the **full polydisc**. Set \(F_\infty=\Psi(0)\). [Webhomes](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/schmid.pdf)

For every positive combination \(M=\sum_j a_jM_j\), the verified nilpotent and sign conditions imply
\[
M^2=0,\qquad
\operatorname{im}M=\ker M=I,\qquad \dim_KI=4.
\]
In particular,
\[
W_0=W_1=I,\qquad W_2=V.
\]

The equality \(\operatorname{im}M=\ker M\) is not assumed from individual ranks: common semidefiniteness and definiteness of the sum make
\[
M:V/I\longrightarrow I
\]
injective, hence bijective.

Restricting to the diagonal \(t_1=\cdots=t_{17}=t\) preserves the same limiting filtration \(F_\infty\). Schmid’s theorem (6.16) then supplies the limiting mixed Hodge structure. Since \(F^0=V_{\mathbb C}\) and \(F^2=0\), Hodge symmetry forces
\[
\operatorname{Gr}_0^W:(0,0),\qquad
\operatorname{Gr}_1^W=0,\qquad
\operatorname{Gr}_2^W:(1,1).
\]
Thus the limit is Hodge–Tate, and
\[
V_\iota=I_\iota\oplus F^1_{\infty,\iota}.
\]
This does **not** assert a splitting over \(\mathbb Q\). [Webhomes](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/schmid.pdf)
