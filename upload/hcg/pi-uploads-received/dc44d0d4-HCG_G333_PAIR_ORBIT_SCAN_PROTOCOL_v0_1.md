## Critical correction: do NOT filter A3 before graph traversal

For rank-one paired tuples let

\[
A3:\quad \operatorname{rank}_K(v_1,\dots,\widehat v_i,\dots,v_6)=3\quad\text{for all }i.
\]

A3 is not invariant under pure pair-conjugation even inside generating G(3,3,3) tuples. Define \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\) and \(s_{ij}(a)\) as the order-2 reflection in \(e_i-\omega^{-a}e_j\). With **one-based** coordinate labels and the six pair-root indices (one-based \(i,j\) but residues \(a\) mod 3), consider

```text
Initial six pair-roots:
(1,3,0), (1,3,0), (1,3,1), (1,3,1), (1,2,2), (1,3,0)

After conjugating pair 4 by pair 5, (s,t) -> (t*s*t,t):
(1,3,0), (1,3,0), (1,3,1), (2,3,2), (1,2,2), (1,3,0)
```

This is the four-strand pure Artin word \(\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\) on the adjacent four punctures (appropriately shifted for pairs 4–5), using the established left-to-right action convention.

Exact results:

- Both tuples generate the same full group, order **54**.
- A3 deletion-rank-3 flags before: `True True True True False True`.
- A3 deletion-rank-3 flags after:  `True True True True True True`.
- The conjugated reflection is checked exactly as a monomial matrix; deletion ranks are checked using exact nonzero 3x3 minors over \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\).

**Consequences:** Retain A3-failing nodes as possible **bridges** in the finite orbit graph. A3 may be used to skip expensive rank evaluation on those nodes, because in the six-pair/nine-curve case any zero pair operator makes rank 9 impossible, but not to remove them from orbit traversal. Filtering only by full generated group is safe, because pair-conjugation preserves that group.

## Safe symmetry reduction

There are 9 reflection lines in G(3,3,3). They form a **single conjugacy class** under the full 54-element group, checked by exact monomial matrices. Simultaneous conjugation preserves H4 rank, the paired structure, and commutes with pair-conjugation. Thus an exhaustive scan **modulo simultaneous global conjugation** may fix the first root reflection, reducing the simple candidate-state upper bound from \(9^6=531441\) to \(9^5=59049\). Further reduction by the stabilizer of that first reflection is optional.

For orbit representatives use deterministic canonicalization under *all* simultaneous G-conjugates, and carefully record this equivalence in the orbit definitions. The quotient describes equivalence classes of local systems; it is not the full pure braid quotient.

## Recommended algorithm

1. Enumerate all six-reflection sequences with generated group G(3,3,3), optionally canonicalizing by simultaneous conjugation. **Do not discard A3 failures from the graph.**
2. Build edges for BOTH adjacent local pure pair-conjugation orientations, validating the exact Artin convention and closure under inverses. Record whether a left or right move is needed to connect each step.
3. Compute connected components of this finite graph; record sizes, counts and exact generator closure/canonicalization checks.
4. On A3-valid states, test the 14 pair-containing trees using the exact root-relation Hermitian edge forms. Since \(m=6\), \(d=3\), and all 9 curves must be independent to achieve rank 9, first apply cheap pair-form rank filters.
5. For **every component** either exhibit an explicit 9-rank witness with tree plus conjugation/pair-move word, or certify complete exhaustion of its eligible nodes and all 14 trees. For positive witnesses, independently verify H0–H4 via actual Artin operators.
6. If a component has no pair-move witness, report **PAIR-SUBGROUP NEGATIVE CANDIDATE**, not a full pure-braid counterexample. Attempt to cross the obstruction with full 12-point pure-braid generators, including paths through unpaired intermediate tuples.

**Do not extrapolate results to arbitrary finite groups or to arbitrary Hurwitz components.**
