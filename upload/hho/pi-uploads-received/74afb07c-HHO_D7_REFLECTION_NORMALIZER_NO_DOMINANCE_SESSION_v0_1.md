## 3. Normal reflection subgroup reduction

Let R be the subgroup generated by **all** rank-one pseudoreflections of Γ. Since conjugates of reflections are reflections, R is normal in Γ. The tuple contains at least sixteen reflections, so R is nontrivial.

The fixed space `V^R` is Γ-invariant. Absolute irreducibility implies `V^R=0`. As a finite complex reflection group, R decomposes orthogonally into irreducible reflection representations `V=⊕_{i=1}^k V_i`. Their summands are distinguished by the direct factors of R and are permuted by Γ; otherwise a sum over Γ-orbits would be Γ-invariant. Hence all blocks have the same dimension. Since 7 is prime, **k=1 or k=7**.

- `k=7`: Γ is monomial. The inherited rank-one induced-period theorem gives `rank dPer <=12<16`, contradicting D.
- `k=1`: R is an irreducible rank-seven reflection group. By Shephard–Todd, R is either imprimitive `G(m,p,7)`, the standard real `W(A7)=S8`, or `W(E7)=G36`.

If R is `G(m,p,7)`, Cohen's normalizer theorem (§2.13, no dimension-seven exceptions) makes Γ monomial, contradicting D. Only the two **real** reflection groups remain.

## 4. No local rank-two element outside the real reflection subgroup

For `R=W(A7)` or `R=W(E7)`, the unitary normalizer is contained in the scalar multiples of the corresponding real root-system automorphism group. Consequently, in an appropriate K-form,

`g = λ w`, with `w∈R`, `λ∈K` a root of unity. To justify `λ∈K`, note that each real reflection representation of `S8` or `W(E7)` is afforded by a rational root lattice; the actual absolutely irreducible K-representation of R is K-equivalent to that rational model (the intertwiner space is the solution of linear equations over K and has a nonzero complex solution). In this basis both g and w have K-entries, so the scalar matrix `λI=gw^{-1}` forces `λ∈K`. Since K=Q(sqrt(-3)), `λ∈μ6`.

- If λ is nonreal, w is real orthogonal, so its λ^{-1}-eigenspace has multiplicity at most floor(7/2)=3 (nonreal eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs). Therefore `c(g)>=4`.
- If λ=−1 and R=E7, then −I∈R, hence g∈R.
- If λ=−1 and R=A7, w acts in the standard seven-dimensional permutation representation of S8; the multiplicity of −1 is the number of even cycles in a permutation of 8 letters, at most 4. Hence `c(g)=7−mult_w(−1)>=3`.
- λ=1 gives g∈R.

Thus **every element g∈Γ with c(g)<=2 belongs to R**.

By §2 at most one tuple generator has rank >=3. All other generators belong to R. If there is one exceptional generator, the product-one relation expresses it as the inverse of the product of the others, so it too belongs to R. The tuple generates Γ; hence **Γ=R**. But R has rational real reflection character and cannot have exact character field K. Contradiction.
