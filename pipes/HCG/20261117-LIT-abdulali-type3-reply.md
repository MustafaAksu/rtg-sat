**[SESSION] LIT's reply on Abdulali's type III theorem.** This is literature support with LIT's own conditional reading. It is not a verdict on Severn v2 Corollary 12.1, not a validation, and claims no novelty. Sources are at rtg-lit `ea7800d3f76a76dee1d3e852f64e3d139d21fb76`, all `stage: candidate`, `disclosure: citable`. "Read" means one co-owner's scoped reading. **[D]** marks LIT deductions.
**Item 1 — the source.** S. Abdulali, *Abelian varieties of type III and the Hodge conjecture*, Internat. J. Math. 10 (1999) 667–675, doi:10.1142/S0129167X99000264. LIT reference: `abdulali1999-type3-hodge@vor-scan`, journal scan read in full.
- **"General"** is defined in the paper (§2.5, p. 670; `U-2`). With G the Hodge group of a generic fiber of the PEL-family, "A is a general fiber … if the Hodge group of A equals G". No countable-union formulation occurs. Severn's reading is the paper's own definition.
- **"Effective"** (§2.5; `U-1`): Γ torsion-free, and End_Q(A) = D for a generic fiber.
- **Theorem 4.1** (p. 673; `Thm 4.1`): for a general member A of an effective PEL-family of type III:
  - the Hodge ring of A is generated by divisors and the Weil cycles of a maximal subfield E of End_Q(A);
  - the algebraicity of those Weil cycles on A implies the usual Hodge conjecture for all powers of A.
  - The stated theorem has no hypothesis on the centre of D, on the parity of m = dim_D V, or on a discriminant.
- **Algebraicity is an input, not proved here.** Theorem 3.2 (`reported`, citing Schoen and van Geemen) gives it for:
  - (a) E = Q(√−3), m = 3, **T split**;
  - (b) E = Q(√−3), m = 2;
  - (c) E = Q(√−1), m = 2, T split.
  The paper does not say which members of a family these cover.
- **Hypotheses for a Severn-type use [D]:**
  - (H1) A is a fiber of an effective type III PEL-family;
  - (H2) Hod(A) equals the generic Hodge group;
  - (H3) the Weil cycles of A for some maximal subfield E are algebraic.
  H1 and H2 are premises of Theorem 4.1. H3 is a further premise, needed for the Hodge-conjecture conclusion. For E = Q(√−3) with m = 3, Theorem 3.2(a) supplies H3 only if T_E is split. Without that, H3 needs another source; the paper does not show it impossible. **For HCG to check:** Severn's family, its restricted form T_E, and whether his Theorem B gives H3.
- **Proof gap, with a conditional repair [D].** In the proofs of Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 the displayed generators are determinants pulled back along the projections A^k → A only. The invariants Weyl's theorem gives also include determinants mixing factors, and σ with σ̄. Projections alone do not produce these.
  - This is a gap in the written proof, not a counterexample: projections alone do not reach the mixed determinants, but nothing here shows the usual-Hodge implication false.
  - Conditional repair ([SESSION], co-owner checked, not a source validation): granting Weyl's first fundamental theorem as cited and D ⊗ C ≅ ∏ M₂(C), the mixed determinants are Q-linear combinations of pullbacks of the Weil cycles along homomorphisms A^k → A, x ↦ Σ d_j x_j with d_j ∈ End(A) (polarisation). On that repair the implication is supported with "Weil cycles" read as **divisors and homomorphism pullbacks, including quaternionic translates**.
- **Dependency on the paper's reference [2].** §2.5 takes "derived groups equal" from its reference [2], §4.6; [2] is not a LIT source. Example 5.1's *general* Hodge conjecture claim cites [2], Theorem 5.1. The author's errata page (`abdulali2011-errata-web@web-20261117:U-1`) restricts that theorem to varieties without type III factors, so that claim lacks its cited support. The *usual*-Hodge statements do not use it.
**Item 2 — later work.**
- Abdulali, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002) 915–928 (`abdulali2002-type3-hodge-structures@arxiv-v1`):
  - Rem. 4.2 restates the 1999 theorem.
  - Cor. 4.3 applies it to *every* simple type III fourfold with √−3 ∈ D, with generality from a Hodge-group computation.
  - The errata page adds "centre of D = Q" to *that paper's* Thm 4.1 (`…errata-web:U-2`). This is not an erratum to the 1999 theorem. The errata page lists none for 1999.
- van Geemen–Verra, arXiv:math/0103111 (`vangeemenverra2001-quaternionic-pryms@arxiv-v1:4.8, 4.9`):
  - §4.8 reads "general" as Hod(A)(C) ≅ SO(2n, C).
  - §4.9: the Weil classes of any quadratic K ⊂ F span the quaternionic classes W_F by F-translates. These are Weil cycles for a maximal subfield, on A itself.
- **Discriminant of the restricted Weil-type structure.** None of the sources read treats it, beyond the split condition in Theorem 3.2(a). The recent literature on algebraicity of Weil classes under discriminant conditions was not read by LIT. This is a coverage gap, not "nothing exists".
