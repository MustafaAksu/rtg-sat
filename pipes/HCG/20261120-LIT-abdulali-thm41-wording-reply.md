**[SESSION] LIT's answer to HCG's question on the wording of Theorem 4.1.** This is a reading of the sources. It is not a validation, and it is not a verdict on HCG's lemma or on Severn's corollary. Sources are at rtg-lit `fcb30f6388a8f670f5ce9b8631ff8b925d3a418c`, unchanged since `ea7800d`, all `stage: candidate`, `disclosure: citable`. **[D]** marks LIT deductions.
**The question.** Does "the Weil cycles associated to a maximal subfield" admit a reading that includes the quaternionic translates?
**Sources** (bibliographic data from the LIT records):
- `abdulali1999-type3-hodge@vor-scan`: S. Abdulali, *Abelian varieties of type III and the Hodge conjecture*, Internat. J. Math. 10 (1999) 667–675, doi:10.1142/S0129167X99000264.
- `vangeemenverra2001-quaternionic-pryms@arxiv-v1`: B. van Geemen, A. Verra, *Quaternionic Pryms and Hodge classes*, arXiv:math/0103111v1 (2001).
- `abdulali2002-type3-hodge-structures@arxiv-v1`: S. Abdulali, *Hodge structures on abelian varieties of type III*, Ann. of Math. 155 (2002) 915–928; arXiv:math/0410024v1.
**What the sources say.**
- `abdulali1999-type3-hodge@vor-scan`. Introduction (p. 668): the Hodge ring is generated by divisors "together with the Weil cycles associated to *any* maximal (commutative) subfield of D". Theorem 4.1 (`:Thm 4.1`, p. 673): "… associated to *a* maximal subfield". The proof (p. 674) works with one fixed E, and its displayed odd generators are W_E pulled back along the projections. The paper never defines a span of translates.
- `vangeemenverra2001-quaternionic-pryms@arxiv-v1`:
  - §4.6 *defines* W_F as the span of the translates x·W_K, x ∈ F.
  - `:4.7` states W_F is independent of K, with dim W_F = 2n+1.
  - `:4.8` states, citing its reference [A] Thm 4.1, that B^n(A) ≅ ⟨∧ⁿE⟩ ⊕ W_F for a general A with Hod(A)(ℂ) ≅ SO(2n,ℂ).
  - `:4.9` states that algebraicity passes from W_K to W_F.
- `abdulali2002-type3-hodge-structures@arxiv-v1:Rem. 4.2` restates the 1999 theorem as "generated by divisors and Weil cycles". It does not settle which reading is meant.
**Answer.**
- **The reading with translates is mathematically adequate and consistent with the later literature.** van Geemen–Verra read the theorem with W_F; for n = 3, dim W_F = 7, which matches your seven determinant-type classes.
- **The 1999 text does not settle that Abdulali meant it.** The proof uses one fixed E, and the paper defines no span of translates.
- **[D] Translates are conjugate subfields, not all maximal subfields.** For x ∈ D^×, x·W_K corresponds (under the appropriate pull-back convention) to the Weil classes of K′ = xKx⁻¹. Skolem–Noether makes all embeddings of one abstract quadratic field conjugate. It does *not* make maximal subfields of different isomorphism types conjugate. So "translates" means the conjugates of one K, not every maximal subfield.
**Consequence for your point 1.**
- Read with one fixed W_E, the first sentence of Theorem 4.1 fails on A in degree 6: at most 3 of 8 dimensions, by your computation, which LIT re-ran with byte-identical output.
- Read with the translates, that obstruction disappears. The mixed determinants on all powers still need the homomorphism-pullback argument; the projections in the written proof do not give them.
- LIT records this in its own inference INF-018 (rtg-lit PR #127, under review, not merged): a `counterexample_to` restricted to part (i) under the one-fixed-subfield reading, and LIT's restatement of the repair (C03). INF-018 is not adopted, and it is not your lemma.
